Nigerian English Accent ASR Fine-Tuning

Overview

Nigerian English is one of the most widely spoken English varieties in Africa, yet it is underrepresented in ASR training data. This project pairs a curated, transcribed Nigerian-English speech corpus with an end-to-end fine-tuning pipeline for NVIDIA’s FastConformer CTC acoustic model, built with NeMo on Google Colab.

The workflow covers the full training lifecycle: dataset conversion and manifests, a corpus-specific tokenizer, a from-scratch baseline that was diagnosed when it failed, fine-tuning of a pretrained model, experiment tracking, and publishing the resulting checkpoint to the Hugging Face Hub.

Dataset & Split

The corpus is Alpha-Proxima/combined-audio, a privately hosted Hugging Face dataset of 1,608 transcribed Nigerian-English utterances with text and duration metadata. It is split 80/20 into training and validation sets with a fixed seed (42). A custom converter turns each Hugging Face sample into a WAV file and a NeMo JSONL manifest entry (audio_filepath, duration, text); NeMo’s duration filtering then removes clips outside the configured bounds. The final training run loaded 1,245 files (2.90 hours) of training audio and 315 files (0.74 hours) for validation.

A SentencePiece BPE tokenizer with a 512-token vocabulary is trained on the transcript corpus using NeMo’s process_asr_text_tokenizer.py, so the model’s output units match the vocabulary of Nigerian-English speech rather than a generic English tokenizer.

Training

Two training runs document the path to a working model. The first was a from-scratch EncDecCTCModelBPE (FastConformer-CTC-BPE configuration, 115 M trainable parameters) with the custom tokenizer. It did not converge: validation WER stayed pinned at 100% and the model emitted empty predictions throughout training. That failure was analysed rather than hidden, and the diagnostics are summarised below.

The working model instead fine-tunes the NGC pretrained stt_en_fastconformer_ctc_large checkpoint on the accent data with PyTorch Lightning and NeMo’s experiment manager: batch size 4, AdamW (learning rate 5e-4, weight decay 0.001), cosine annealing with 2,000 warmup steps, SpecAugment, and 16-bit mixed precision on a single Colab GPU. Checkpoints are selected by minimum validation WER with early stopping (patience 3), and runs are logged to Weights & Biases (project naija_en_asr).

Results

Validation WER fell from 55.1% at epoch 0 to 28.1% at epoch 12, improving at every saved checkpoint (0.551 → 0.430 → 0.377 → 0.360 → 0.348 → 0.334 → 0.304 → 0.288 → 0.281); training stopped after 15 epochs. The best checkpoint — a 463 MB NeMo archive — was pushed to the Hugging Face Hub as oegbo/fastconformer_enng and reloaded from the Hub for a further two epochs of continued fine-tuning.

Diagnosing the Failed Baseline

The notebook documents the analysis behind the empty predictions: a 128-token vocabulary from an earlier tokenizer build that was too small for the corpus’s linguistic diversity, an undertrained model, learning-rate and optimisation settings, and data-quality checks on the audio and transcriptions. Each is paired with a concrete next step — a larger vocabulary (the final tokenizer uses 512 tokens), longer training, and a reviewed learning-rate schedule.
