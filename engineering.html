Selected Work

Which of 443,455 radio sources are stars?

The MeerKAT Galactic Plane Survey catalogues 443,455 compact radio sources. Most are not stars at all — they are background galaxies, quasars and active galactic nuclei. I needed the stellar ones, matched against Gaia DR3 (1.81 billion optical sources) and X-ray catalogues.

Nearest-neighbour matching inside a distance limit was the obvious first move. A Monte Carlo null model — randomising positions and re-matching — showed why it fails: reliability stayed under about 15% at every radius tested. Restricting candidates by Bayesian distance estimates fixed the purity (91% at 50 pc) but returned six stars. Accurate, and useless.

What worked was changing the reference catalogue instead of the radius: assemble ~361,000 Gaia objects from populations already known to emit radio — flare stars, RS CVn and BY Dra binaries, young stellar objects, OB and Wolf–Rayet stars — then match against that and quantify it with the same null model. The result was 629 stellar counterparts at 94% reliability within 2 arcseconds, published in MNRAS.

Medical speech on a free GPU

I wanted to see how much of the medical-ASR gap a small model could close on a free Colab GPU. whisper-medium crashed before 500 steps, so I used whisper-small: 1,000 steps, batch 32 — and I was pleased that batch 32 fit at all.

The data fought back first. The source dataset’s split was odd — most of the audio sat in the “test” partition — so I resplit it, kept 500 clips untouched, capped clips at Whisper’s 30-second window, and resampled everything to 16 kHz mono.

The evaluation harness earned its keep on the first run: the stock baseline beat my fine-tuned model. Two bugs. Test audio was going in at its native 44.1 kHz to a pipeline that assumed 16 kHz, so the model was hearing mush; and unconstrained decoding produced repetition loops that dominated the word error rate. Resampling the test set and applying the same constrained decoding to both models fixed both.

The honest number, 500 untouched clips: 11.0% → 5.4% normalized WER (20.6% → 12.7% raw). The checkpoint is on Hugging Face and the notebook is public. What I would do next is the harder part: a speaker-disjoint clinical test set, and error rates on drug names and dosages rather than only normalized text.

A search engine for the literature nobody reads in full

Astrophysics publishes more than any researcher can read. This service ingests arXiv papers, parses the full PDFs with Docling, and answers questions with citations back to the source: Airflow for monthly ingestion, FastAPI and Gradio on top, and OpenSearch, Postgres, Redis, MinIO, Ollama and Langfuse running in Docker Compose.

The one real design decision was hybrid retrieval. Keyword search misses paraphrases; vector search misses exact identifiers and terminology, which is most of scientific text. Both live in a single OpenSearch index — BM25 alongside HNSW over 768-dimension nomic-embed-text vectors — fused with reciprocal rank fusion. If the embedding service fails at query time, the API falls back to BM25 instead of erroring.

Generation runs on a local 1B-parameter model constrained to the retrieved excerpts, with citations required and uncertainty allowed. Every request is traced in Langfuse, so a bad answer can be traced to retrieval or to the model. The gap I am honest about: there is no retrieval benchmark yet — no gold question set, so recall and citation precision are not measured. Building that harness is the next step.
